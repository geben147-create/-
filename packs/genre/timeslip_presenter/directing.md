# TIMESLIP_PRESENTER — directing notes

Everything is present tense. The presenter is here now, discovering as we watch.
Past-tense narration ("I had gone to...") collapses the premise, because the
appeal is that the outcome is not yet known.

Only the presenter may look at the lens; that gaze is the device that separates
them from the world. When a period character also looks at us the boundary is
gone and the setting turns into a stage set.

Keep the presenter's clothing, hair and props identical shot to shot — the
character lock is doing most of the work of making this feel like one continuous
visit rather than a series of images.

Rhythm sits around 3 seconds: fast enough to carry a running commentary, slow
enough that the period detail is legible. Ease slightly into the reaction shots;
a reaction cut too early reads as a mistake rather than a joke.
